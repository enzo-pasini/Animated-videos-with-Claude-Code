# Facts — Mira's foggy glasses

## F1 — Glasses fog when cold lenses cool warm, moist air below its dew point: the water vapor condenses into tiny droplets on the lens.
status: confirmed
- source: https://www.usgs.gov/media/images/water-condensing-chilled-eye-glasses-can-be-annoying — U.S. Geological Survey, Water Science School (public domain)
  quote: "the lenses fog up as small water droplets coat the surface via condensation."
  quote: "All of these are examples of water leaving the vapor state in the warm air and condensing into liquid as it is cools."
- source: https://forecast.weather.gov/glossary.php?word=Dew — NOAA National Weather Service, Glossary
  quote: "Moisture that has condensed on objects near the ground, whose temperatures have fallen below the dewpoint temperature."
  quote: "It is the temperature to which air must be cooled in order to reach saturation (assuming air pressure and moisture content are constant)."

Note: the USGS example goes the other way (cold room → humid outside), but it states the same mechanism: vapor in
warm air condenses into droplets on a chilled lens. The script only says "cold lenses chill warm air" → "vapor turns
to droplets", which both sources support.
