## 2026-11-12 — "a fictional, intriguing story" (Faça um video envolvendo alguma história ficticia, intrigante): 5 ideas

All in the explainer format: an invented character lives a mystery, the ending reveals the true mechanism.
(Pure fiction with no real fact would need the reserved `story` format, not built yet.)

1. **The ghosts came from the furnace** — hook type `paradox`
   - Hook: "The ghosts in this house came from the furnace."
   - Odd detail: in a 1921 medical report, a whole family heard footsteps and saw figures at night; it stopped when a broken furnace was fixed.
   - Mechanism: a leaking furnace fills the house with carbon monoxide; it sticks to blood far more strongly than oxygen, so the brain runs short of oxygen and makes headaches, fear and hallucinations.
   - Places: a home at night (home kit, warmInterior); the basement furnace + an insert of blood cells.
   - → picked: videos/2026-11-12-furnace-ghosts/
2. **The bowl of water that heard the enemy** — hook type `strange-act`
   - Hook: "A bowl of water warned the castle at midnight."
   - Odd detail: defenders set bowls of water (or dry peas on a drum) on the floor near the walls and watched them.
   - Mechanism: enemies digging a tunnel under the wall shake the ground; the tiny shaking travels through rock and makes ripples on still water.
   - Places: a castle under siege at night (medieval kit); the tunnel under the wall.
3. **Three climbers, but they counted four** — hook type `strange-act`
   - Hook: "Three men crossed the ice, and all three counted four."
   - Odd detail: Shackleton wrote that on his 1916 crossing of South Georgia he often felt they were four, not three.
   - Mechanism (soft: "scientists think"): under extreme cold, tiredness and fear the brain can project a sense of "someone beside you" — the "third man factor".
   - Places: a snowy pass (snow kit, dayExterior); the mountain hut at the end.
4. **The star that vanishes when you look at it** — hook type `look-at-these`
   - Hook: "This star disappears every time you look straight at it."
   - Odd detail: a night watchman can only see it by looking a little to the side.
   - Mechanism: the very center of the eye has only color cells (cones), which need lots of light; the dim-light cells (rods) sit around it, so a faint star shows up only off-center ("averted vision").
   - Places: a night street / rooftop (night_street); an insert of the eye.
5. **Snow-blind on a cloudy day** — hook type `you-scene`
   - Hook: "Your eyes can get sunburned under thick clouds."
   - Odd detail: the climber feels fine all day, then goes blind about 6–12 hours later, in the dark hut.
   - Mechanism: UV light passes through clouds and snow reflects most of it back up into the eyes; it burns the clear front of the eye (photokeratitis), and the pain arrives hours later.
   - Places: a snowy summit (snow kit); the mountain hut at night (warmInterior).

## 2026-11-07 — first full video (35–60 s): 5 ideas

1. **Soldiers break step on bridges** — hook type `forbidden`
   - Hook: "It's forbidden to march in step on this bridge."
   - Odd detail: the Broughton Suspension Bridge (near Manchester) fell under marching soldiers in 1831; London's Albert Bridge still has a sign asking troops to break step.
   - Mechanism: steps in time push the bridge at its own sway rhythm, so each push adds to the last swing (resonance).
   - Places: a bridge in daylight (dayExterior); soldiers marching up the road.
   - Note: the 1831 fall may also have had a weak bolt; soften if the sources say so.
   - → picked: videos/2026-11-07-bridge-break-step/
2. **Why surgeons wear green** — hook type `strange-act`
   - Hook: "Surgeons kept seeing green ghosts on the white walls."
   - Odd detail: around 1914 one surgeon changed white gowns to green (date not checked).
   - Mechanism: staring at red blood tires the eye's red-sensing cells, so white looks green-blue for a moment (an afterimage); green cloth hides it.
   - Places: an operating room (warmInterior); a close-up insert of the eye.
3. **Pasta that won't cook on a mountain** — hook type `you-scene`
   - Hook: "Your pasta can stay hard in boiling water."
   - Odd detail: on top of Everest, water boils at about 70 °C, not 100 °C.
   - Mechanism: higher up, less air presses on the water, so it boils at a lower temperature, and boiling water is cooler.
   - Places: a snowy peak camp (dayExterior); a mountain hut kitchen (warmInterior).
4. **Wrinkly fingers need working nerves** — hook type `question`
   - Hook: "Why do wet fingers wrinkle only when their nerves work?"
   - Odd detail: doctors used the wrinkle as a test for nerve damage (cut nerve = no wrinkles).
   - Mechanism: it isn't water soaking in; nerves tell the blood vessels in the fingertip to tighten, and the skin folds (maybe for better grip — not proven).
   - Places: a bathtub (warmInterior); a doctor's office + an insert diagram.
5. **Lemon stops apples browning** — hook type `look-at-these`
   - Hook: "These two apple slices: one browns in minutes, one stays white."
   - Odd detail: the white one only had a few drops of lemon.
   - Mechanism: cutting breaks the cells, an enzyme meets the air and turns the apple's chemicals brown; lemon's acid and vitamin C stop it.
   - Places: a kitchen (warmInterior); an orchard or market stall (dayExterior).
