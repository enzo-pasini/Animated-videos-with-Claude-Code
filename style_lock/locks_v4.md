# STYLE LOCK — v4 (effects + character looks)

**v4 = the locked v3 look (`locks.md`) + approved character looks + approved visual effects (VFX).**
Two kinds of additions:
- **Looks** are **always on**: they change how every character is drawn, on purpose (the user's choice).
  The regression baseline is now **`v4cloth`** = L1 + L3 + L2 (`style_lock/frames/v4cloth_f*.png`, `style_test_v4cloth.mp4`;
  `locks.json` → `regress_baseline`; `v4id` = L1 + L3 and `v4look` = L1 only, kept as history). The v3 frames stay: with the look off
  (`"look": null`) the drawing must still match v3, round heads included
  (`regress_frames.py --baseline v3 --props tools/tests/look_off.json`).
- **Effects** are **off by default** and switched on per shot by the scene plan.

## Rules for using effects

- An effect is a **story beat**, not decoration: use it when the script says the feeling (e.g. "his face goes purple"). Start it on that word.
- Anger shouts with the mouth: fine when the character "says" an angry line in the script. Avoid long anger holds under neutral narration.
- A hand that holds a prop raises the prop with the gesture (Mira shakes her clipboard).
- `heat` needs a free hand for the cloth: it uses the hand that holds nothing (a new part that holds a prop must set `holds`). Use it when the script says the heat or the tiredness ("summer comes", "after ten hours of work"). The heat itself (hot colors, hard light) is the set's job.
- `sweat` is a badge for nervous or shocked moments: put it on top of an expression (`expr` step) on the word of the shock.
- 3D face expressions are **short beats**, like the reference: hold each one **10–30 frames** and chain them (each starts from where the one before ended, so the face turns on smoothly). Start it on the word of the feeling. A long hold (~60 frames) is rare.
- They need a **medium or close shot**: in a wide shot the turn is too small to read.
- `think` and `shy` use the free hand / both hands: a prop in the busy hand moves with it (Mira's clipboard comes to the front in `shy`).
- **Jump** (`jump` channel) is a story beat too: a jump for joy, a fright, waking up with a start. Pair it with a face on the same word (`scared` for a fright, `happy` / `laugh` for joy, `shock`…). It needs the whole body and headroom in the shot (feet + ground visible; the big jump goes up 1.35 head widths). The jump sets the arms while it runs.
- **Edge lines** (`lines` + camera `punch`) **intensify** a strong moment: the user's rule (2026-11-11) — great for **anger** and a character **shouting / getting worked up**, also a shock. Put them on the word of the outburst, together with the face fx (`anger`, `shock`, `scared`). A short beat (15 frames), not under neutral narration; at most a few per video so it keeps its punch.
- No camera shake on a landing (tested, dropped by the user 2026-11-10; the v3 "No shake" rule stays).
- **Walk** (`walk` actor field) moves a character from one mark to another. Pick the **pace** from the scene: `normal` for calm walking, `fast` for hurry, `rush` for urgency or panic (the user's rule, 2026-11-11: fast walks bring variety and show urgency, hurry or anguish). Feet + ground visible; walking toward or away from the camera reads best (the card always faces the camera). To travel with the walker, use a raw camera move with `drift` = the pace speed (normal 1.0, fast 1.6, rush 2.2 m/s) along the walk. The face is free; the arms are not changed.
- Not part of the effect (later): lip sync for a character who speaks. A push-in on a shock: camera `punch` (with the edge lines, item 5).
