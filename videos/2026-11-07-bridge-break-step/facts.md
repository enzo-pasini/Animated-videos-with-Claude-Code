# Facts — Soldiers break step on bridges

## F1 — After the 1831 Broughton collapse, the British Army ordered troops to "break step" when crossing a bridge.
status: confirmed
- source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broughton_Suspension_Bridge — Wikipedia, "Broughton Suspension Bridge"
  quote: "As a result of the incident, the British Army issued an order that troops should "break step" when crossing a bridge."
- source: https://manchestervictorianarchitects.org.uk/buildings/suspension-bridge-broughton — The Manchester Group of the Victorian Society (cites Drewery, "A Memoir on Suspension Bridges", 1832)
  quote: "As a result of this collapse, the British Army issued an order that troops should "break step" when crossing a weak bridge."
- source: https://parabola.unsw.edu.au/sites/default/files/2024-02/vol57_no1_3.pdf — Parabola, UNSW School of Mathematics and Statistics, vol. 57 no. 1
  quote: "In the case of soldiers, squadrons are ordered to break stride to prevent marching in unison while crossing the bridge." (the PDF splits "ordered" over a line break)

## F2 — London's Albert Bridge has signs at its entrances telling troops to break step, and they are still in place.
status: confirmed
- source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Bridge,_London — Wikipedia, "Albert Bridge, London"
  quote: "Nicknamed "The Trembling Lady" because of its tendency to vibrate when large numbers of people walked over it, the bridge has signs at its entrances that warn troops to break step whilst crossing the bridge."
  quote: "Concerns about the risks of mechanical resonance effects on suspension bridges, following the 1831 collapse of the Broughton Suspension Bridge and the 1850 collapse of Angers Bridge, led to notices being placed at the entrances warning troops to break step (i.e. not to march in rhythm) when crossing the bridge; Although the barracks closed in 2008, the warning signs are still in place."

Note: the ICE page (https://www.ice.org.uk/what-is-civil-engineering/infrastructure-projects/albert-bridge) says the current signs date from after 1965 (replacements of the Victorian ones). It blocks scripts (403), so it is not quoted. The script only says the sign "still" says so, which both readings support.

## F3 — On 12 April 1831, 74 soldiers marching four abreast over the Broughton Suspension Bridge (Salford, Greater Manchester) felt it vibrate in time with their steps.
status: confirmed
- source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broughton_Suspension_Bridge — Wikipedia
  quote: "On 12 April 1831, the bridge collapsed, reportedly due to mechanical resonance induced by troops marching in step."
  quote: "As a detachment of 74 men returned to barracks in Salford by way of the bridge, the soldiers, who were marching four abreast, felt it begin to vibrate in time with their footsteps."
  quote: "Broughton Suspension Bridge was an iron chain suspension bridge built in 1826 to span the River Irwell between Broughton and Pendleton, now in Salford, Greater Manchester, England."
- source: https://manchestervictorianarchitects.org.uk/buildings/suspension-bridge-broughton — The Manchester Group of the Victorian Society
  quote: "The Broughton Bridge was broken down on the 12th April, 1831, by a party of soldiers marching A over it in step." (the stray "A" is in the page text)

Note: other sites give 60 or "about 70" men; 74 is the figure in Wikipedia, which follows the 1831 reports.

## F4 — Some soldiers found the shaking funny, whistled a marching tune and stepped along with it, so it shook even more.
status: confirmed
- source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broughton_Suspension_Bridge — Wikipedia
  quote: "Finding the vibration amusing, some of them started to whistle a marching tune, and to "humour it by the manner in which they stepped", causing the bridge to vibrate even more."

Note: one source only (an anecdote from the 1831 accounts). The script says "some of the men", as the source does.

## F5 — A corner of the bridge fell into the river, throwing about forty soldiers into water only about two feet deep; nobody died, twenty were injured.
status: confirmed
- source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broughton_Suspension_Bridge — Wikipedia
  quote: "The corner of the bridge, no longer supported, then fell 16 or 18 feet (4.9 or 5.5 m) into the river, throwing about forty of the soldiers into the water or against the chains."
  quote: "The river was low and the water only about two feet (60 cm) deep at that point."
  quote: "None of the men were killed, but twenty were injured, including six who suffered severe injuries including broken arms and legs, severe bruising, and contusions to the head."

## F6 — The investigation found a badly forged bolt had snapped; the marching vibration made it fail, though it would have failed eventually anyway.
status: confirmed
- source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broughton_Suspension_Bridge — Wikipedia
  quote: "The report criticised the construction method used, as the attachment to the ground anchor relied on a single bolt (rather than two), and the bolt was found to have been badly forged."
  quote: "The conclusion of the investigation was that the vibration caused by the marching precipitated the bolt's failure, but that it would have failed eventually anyway."
- source: https://manchestervictorianarchitects.org.uk/buildings/suspension-bridge-broughton — The Manchester Group of the Victorian Society (quoting Drewery, 1832)
  quote: "Whence it must be inferred that the shocks, and the vibration given to the bridge by the simultaneous stepping of the men, must have produced an extra strain of about 30 tons beyond that of the mere load, in order to break the holding bolt."

## F7 — Mechanism (resonance): a bridge, like a swing, has a natural frequency; pushes that arrive in time with it (footsteps in unison) make each swing bigger than the last.
status: confirmed
- source: https://parabola.unsw.edu.au/sites/default/files/2024-02/vol57_no1_3.pdf — Parabola, UNSW School of Mathematics and Statistics
  quote: "All physical objects exist with a natural frequency."
  quote: "The child is pushed at the same point during each oscillate, and the toddler will continue to swing higher and higher."
  quote: "The physics of the oscillations of a bridge caused by the marching of soldiers is comparable to the physics of the oscillations of a toddler on a swing being pushed by a person."
  quote: "In the case of a resonant reaction, the amplitude of the oscillations would continue to increase indeﬁnitely, until either the pedestrians cease their footsteps or the bridge breaks and collapses." (the "ﬁ" ligature is in the PDF text)
- source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Bridge,_London — Wikipedia
