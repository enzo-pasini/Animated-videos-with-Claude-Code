# The Bridge That Hated Marching
format: explainer · target: 50 s · pov: you · hook: forbidden · you-see: late · ending: button · result: forbidden to march in step

## Script
[hook] Soldiers are forbidden to march in step here. [F2]
A sign on London's Albert Bridge still says so. [F2]
[escalation] It sounds like a silly rule. [story]
Now you're one of seventy-four soldiers near Manchester, in eighteen thirty-one. [F3]
You march four abreast onto the Broughton Suspension Bridge. [F3]
Then the bridge starts to bounce, in time with your boots. [F3]
Some of the men think it's funny. [F4]
So they whistle a marching tune and step along with the bounce. [F4]
It shakes even harder. [F4]
Then one corner drops, and about forty men fall into the river. [F5]
Luckily, it's only two feet deep. [F5]
Nobody dies, but twenty men are hurt. [F5]
[turn] Investigators found a badly made bolt that had snapped. [F6]
But the marching is what snapped it that day. [F6]
[you-see] You see, every bridge has its own natural swing, like a playground swing. [F7]
[reveal] Push a swing at the right moment, every time, and it goes higher and higher. [F7]
Boots landing together are those pushes, so each step adds to the last bounce. [F7]
[ending] That's why the army still says: break step. [F1]
Walk like a crowd, not a drum. [story]
