Soldiers are forbidden to march in step here.
A sign on London's Albert Bridge still says so.
It sounds like a silly rule.
Now you're one of seventy-four soldiers near Manchester, in eighteen thirty-one.
You march four abreast onto the Broughton Suspension Bridge.
Then the bridge starts to bounce, in time with your boots.
Some of the men think it's funny.
So they whistle a marching tune and step along with the bounce.
It shakes even harder.
Then one corner drops, and about forty men fall into the river.
Luckily, it's only two feet deep.
Nobody dies, but twenty men are hurt.
Investigators found a badly made bolt that had snapped.
But the marching is what snapped it that day.
You see, every bridge has its own natural swing, like a playground swing.
Push a swing at the right moment, every time, and it goes higher and higher.
Boots landing together are those pushes, so each step adds to the last bounce.
That's why the army still says: break step.
Walk like a crowd, not a drum.
