Lena's ghosts were stopped by a furnace repairman.
It started the week her family moved into the old house.
At night, Lena heard slow footsteps upstairs, where nobody was.
Then her mother woke to a man in a dark coat standing at the foot of her bed.
Every morning, the whole family woke up tired, with the same dull headache.
Even the houseplants started dying.
So her dad brought in a priest, who blessed every room.
That night, the footsteps came right back, louder than before.
Lena started sleeping with the lights on.
But Lena noticed something odd: the ghosts only came on cold nights, when the furnace was burning.
On warm nights, the house was quiet, and nobody had a headache.
You see, the old furnace was broken, leaking its fumes into the rooms.
Carbon monoxide: no color, no smell.
It grabs the blood's oxygen carriers about two hundred times harder than oxygen does.
So the brain starves, and a starving brain can see ghosts.
One repair later, the ghosts were gone.
In nineteen twenty-one, a doctor reported a real family just like Lena's.
