# Facts — The Soup That Heats Itself

## F1 — Cookware in a microwave is often much cooler than the food; the hot food warms it only somewhat.
status: softened
say: "the bowl is barely warm" / "the bowl only warms up later, from the hot soup touching it"
- source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave_oven — Wikipedia, "Microwave oven"
  quote: "Cookware used in a microwave oven is often much cooler than the food because the cookware is transparent to microwaves, which penetrate the cookware and heat only the food, although the hot food heats the cookware to some extent."

Note: softened because it depends on the bowl ("often"). Some ceramics do absorb a little, so the script says "barely warm", not "cold".

## F2 — Microwaves are electromagnetic waves similar to radio waves, made inside the oven and reflected around its metal interior.
status: confirmed
- source: https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/resources-you-radiation-emitting-products/microwave-ovens — U.S. Food and Drug Administration, "Microwave Ovens"
  quote: "Microwave ovens heat food using microwaves, a form of electromagnetic radiation similar to radio waves."
  quote: "The microwaves are reflected within the metal interior of the oven where they are absorbed by food."
  quote: "Microwaves are produced inside the oven by an electron tube called a magnetron."

## F3 — A water molecule has a positive end and a negative end; the waves' changing electric field tugs at them and makes the molecule rotate.
status: confirmed
- source: https://www.mcgill.ca/oss/article/general/device-causes-water-molecules-flip-back-and-forth-some-245-billion-times-second-what-it — McGill University, Office for Science and Society
  quote: "Since electrons have a greater affinity for oxygen than hydrogen, the oxygen end of the molecule bears a negative charge while the hydrogen end is positively charged."
  quote: "Then as the waves pass through water, the oscillating positive and negative fields tug at the positive and negative parts of the water molecule causing them to rotate."
- source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave_oven — Wikipedia
  quote: "Dielectric heating takes advantage of the electric dipole structure of water molecules, fats, and many other substances in the food."
  quote: "In an alternating electric field, they will rotate as they continually try to align themselves to the field."

## F4 — Home microwave ovens work at about 2.45 GHz, so water molecules flip back and forth about 2.45 billion times a second.
status: confirmed
- source: https://www.mcgill.ca/oss/article/general/device-causes-water-molecules-flip-back-and-forth-some-245-billion-times-second-what-it — McGill University, Office for Science and Society
  quote: "This device causes water molecules to flip back and forth some 2.45 billion times a second."
- source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave_oven — Wikipedia
  quote: "Typically, consumer ovens work around a nominal 2.45 gigahertz (GHz) – a wavelength of 12.2 centimetres (4.80 in) in the 2.4 GHz to 2.5 GHz ISM band – while large industrial / commercial ovens often use 915 megahertz (MHz) – 32.8 centimetres (12.9 in)."

Note: the script says "about two and a half billion".

## F5 — The moving water molecules bump into their neighbours and make them move faster too; that faster motion is heat.
status: confirmed
- source: https://www.mcgill.ca/oss/article/general/device-causes-water-molecules-flip-back-and-forth-some-245-billion-times-second-what-it — McGill University, Office for Science and Society
  quote: "The agitated water molecules bump into whatever molecules happen to be nearby causing these to increase their random motion, and presto, we have heat."
  quote: "Indeed, heat is nothing more than a reflection of the increased motion of molecules."
- source: https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/resources-you-radiation-emitting-products/microwave-ovens — U.S. Food and Drug Administration
  quote: "This vibration results in friction between molecules, which produces heat that cooks the food."

## F6 — Microwaves pass through glass, ceramic, paper and plastic; that is why those containers are used.
status: confirmed
- source: https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/resources-you-radiation-emitting-products/microwave-ovens — U.S. Food and Drug Administration
  quote: "Glass, paper, ceramic, or plastic containers are used in microwave cooking because microwaves pass through these materials."
  quote: "Microwaves have three characteristics that allow them to be used in cooking: they are reflected by metal; they pass through glass, paper, plastic, and similar materials; and they are absorbed by foods."
- source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave_oven — Wikipedia (same sentence as F1: "the cookware is transparent to microwaves")

## F7 — The heat is made directly in the food, but the waves only reach about 1 cm in; the inside heats by conduction from the hot outer layers.
status: confirmed
- source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave_oven — Wikipedia
  quote: "Microwave ovens produce heat directly within the food, but despite the common misconception that microwaved food cooks from the inside out, 2.45 GHz microwaves can only penetrate approximately 1 cm (0.4 in) into most foods."
- source: https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/resources-you-radiation-emitting-products/microwave-ovens — U.S. Food and Drug Administration
  quote: "When thick foods are cooked, the outer layers are heated and cooked primarily by microwaves while the inside is cooked mainly by the conduction of heat from the hot outer layers."
