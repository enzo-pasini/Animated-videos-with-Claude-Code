# The Soup That Heats Itself
format: explainer · target: 55 s · pov: she · hook: strange-act · you-see: early · ending: button · result: the bowl is barely warm

## Script
[hook] Steaming soup, but the bowl is barely warm. [F1]
[escalation] Nina touches the bowl, sips the soup, and asks why. [story]
[you-see] You see, a microwave fills its metal box with invisible waves, cousins of radio waves. [F2]
But why do those waves make soup hot?
Because soup is full of water, and every water molecule has a slightly positive end and a slightly negative end. [F3]
The waves tug at those ends, so the molecule twists to follow them. [F3]
It flips back and forth about two and a half billion times a second. [F4]
And why does all that flipping make heat?
Because flipping molecules bump into their neighbors, and heat is just molecules moving faster. [F5]
So the soup makes its own heat, from its own water. [F7]
[turn] Then why is the bowl barely warm?
Because the waves pass right through glass and ceramic, like light through a window. [F6]
[reveal] The waves skip the bowl and shake only the water in the soup. [F1]
The bowl only warms up later, from the hot soup touching it. [F1]
And the waves only reach about a centimeter in, so the middle heats from the outside layers. [F7]
[ending] The microwave shakes the water. The water does the cooking. [F5]
